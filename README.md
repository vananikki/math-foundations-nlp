Natural Language Processing (NLP), a pivotal discipline within Artificial Intelligence, en
deavors to enable computers to comprehend and generate human language. This complex
task necessitates the transformation of inherently ambiguous linguistic data into structured,
machine-processable formats. This paper introduces the foundational roles and specific appli
cations of four critical mathematical domains—Logic and Proof, Number Theory, Counting,
and Graph and Tree Theory—within NLP. It explores how these disciplines underpin various
NLP paradigms, from historical rule-based systems and statistical models to contemporary
deep learning architectures and large language models. Specifically, this report delves into
the indispensable role of formal logic and proof theory, from their historical origins to their
integration with modern computational paradigms. It analyzes in detail the application of
logic in key NLP tasks, the inherent challenges, and promising future directions, emphasizing
the ongoing evolution towards more robust, explainable, and human-like AI systems through
the synergistic integration of logic and learning
